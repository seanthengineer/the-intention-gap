# Scientific Audit: The Intention Gap

Independent verification of the M01–M03 results, carried out 2026-11-18. The audit changed no published metric, outcome rule, model or dashboard figure. Two verification scripts were added: `audit/verify_outcomes.py` and `audit/verify_models.py`. Neither imports the `analysis` package, and both print aggregate output only.

**Severity scale:**
- **Major:** could change a headline claim or its interpretation.
- **Moderate:** a published statement is inaccurate or overstated, but the result stands.
- **Minor:** documentation or robustness gap.

## 2. Outcome accuracy

`audit/verify_outcomes.py` rebuilds the variable map from the codebook and reclassifies every eligible woman from the raw CSV using the written rules in `docs/methodology.md` section 3.

- **Eligibility reproduced:** 8,984 respondents; 4,385 women; 3,774 women interviewed in 2009. Of those, 3,660 gave a valid answer, 3 refused and 111 said they did not know.
- **Labels reproduced exactly:** 1,234 positive, 2,170 negative, 256 unknown, with 3,660 of 3,660 labels and all seven unknown-reason counts identical (219 / 4 / 2 / 2 / 9 / 8 / 12).
- **Discrepancy found and resolved:** my first implementation matched pre-baseline children by exact date and produced 11 extra unknowns: 7 pipeline negatives and 4 pipeline positives. Rule 4 of the methodology reconciles by count: a changed date with an unchanged count is not a conflict. With rule 4 implemented as written, the results agree exactly. The strict date-matching reading is a defensible alternative that M02.1 did not test.
- **Boundary months:**
  - 25 women have a follow-up birth dated in the baseline month. Some match the 2009 roster; 8 remain unknown as unmatched.
  - 25 women have a birth dated in the anniversary month; 12 of them are unknown because of it. The rest are labelled through another qualifying birth or are unknown for another reason.
- **Implausible dates:** across rounds, 3–5 recorded births per round occur before the mother was 13. One is recorded at maternal age 0.8, which is impossible. All are before baseline, so they affect the parity feature but no outcome label.

## 3. Population and selection validity

**Official guidance checked:**
- [Sample weights and design effects](https://www.nlsinfo.org/content/cohorts/nlsy97/using-and-understanding-the-data/sample-weights-design-effects)
- [Custom weights](https://www.nlsinfo.org/content/weights/nlsy97)
- [Custom weighting program documentation](https://nlsinfo.org/content/weights/custom-weighting-program-documentation)

What the guidance requires:
- Multi-round analyses need custom weights for the selected rounds, or for a specific list of respondents.
- Design-based standard errors need `VSTRAT` and `VPSU`, which are not in the extract.
- Weights do not correct for item nonresponse.

**Selection into the analytical cohort:**
- 611 of 4,385 women (13.9%) were not interviewed in 2009.
- 114 of the interviewed women did not give a number.
- 256 of the eligible women (7.0%) have an unknown outcome.

**Differential follow-up** (unknown share among eligible women):

|---|---|
| By stated answer | 6.5%–8.4% (flat) |
| Black | 5.1% |
| Hispanic | 7.7% |
| Non-Black, non-Hispanic | 8.4% |
| Cross-sectional sample | 7.8% |
| Oversample | 6.0% |
| Childless at baseline | 8.4% |
| With children | 5.9% |

- Weighted with the Round 13 weight, the unknown share is 7.9% and the mean stated chance is 44.1 (labelled) vs 41.8 (unknown).

**What can be concluded:** results describe the analytical cohort, defined as women interviewed in Round 13 who gave a number and were followed to 2015 or 2017. They are unweighted and ignore strata and PSUs. Population-level estimates for U.S. women born 1980–84 are not supported until custom longitudinal weights and the design variables are obtained. Obtaining those weights through the respondent-list option means submitting public-use IDs, which needs the owner's approval. This audit did not submit them.

| CV D − C AUC: mean, folds | +0.0122, 48/50 | +0.0121, **47/50** |

**Pipeline review:**
- **Features:** all are measured at or before the 2009–10 interview.
- **Preprocessing:** imputation and scaling sit inside the pipeline and are fitted on training data only.
- **No leakage:** label-provenance columns and identifiers are excluded by an allowlist.
- **Uncertainty:** the bootstrap resamples whole households and pairs models on the same resamples.

**Cross-validation intervals:** the folds share training data, and a naive t-interval that ignores this is far too narrow (Brier [−0.0065, −0.0052]). A t-interval on the 10 repeat means is narrower still, because the repeats reuse the same data. The project's Nadeau–Bengio corrected interval (1/50 + 0.25)·s² does account for the dependence, but it is an approximate correction. It was derived for random train/test resampling of independent observations, not for grouped folds. It should be described as approximate, not exact.

**Held-out vs exploratory:** the held-out split is the confirmatory evaluation, run once with pre-specified features and no tuning. The repeated cross-validation was run after the held-out results were known and covers only the training partition. It is exploratory.

## 5. Challenging the main finding

The claim under test: *stated fertility expectations contain useful predictive information but are too extreme, and simple recalibration improves probability accuracy.*

**Supporting evidence:**
- The calculations reproduce independently.
- Observed birth rates rise steadily with the stated answer.
- The answers are too extreme under every outcome definition tested: 14 scenarios, the strict-date variant and the early-birth bound.
- Recalibration improves held-out Brier by 0.051 [0.038, 0.063], and beats the raw answers in every cross-validation fold.

**Weakening evidence and alternative explanations:**
1. **Known pregnancies (V1).** Part of the "information" is women reporting pregnancies they already knew about. This weakens the discrimination claim but strengthens the extremeness claim.
2. **Reporting behaviour, not belief.** Heaping at 0, 50 and 100 and the five-category AUC suggest that answers express categorical intentions ("no / unsure / yes"). Under classical reporting noise, an OLS slope is attenuated even if underlying beliefs were calibrated. "Too extreme" is therefore established for the *reported numbers as forecasts*, not for women's beliefs.
3. **Period effects.** The 2009–2015 window coincides with a sharp national fertility decline after the 2008 recession. The 5-point average overestimate may partly reflect unforeseeable conditions rather than biased expectations. One cohort and one period cannot separate these.
4. **Attrition.** The average overestimate ranges from 1.4 to 7.6 points under the missing-data bounds.
5. **Construct differences.** Adoption may be included in the question but not in the outcome. "In five years" may be read as a point in time. Pregnancy loss is unobserved. All of these add noise that makes the answers look more extreme.
6. **Transportability.** The recalibration map (0% → 17%, 100% → 66%) is fitted to this cohort and period. Its value for other cohorts is untested.

**Analyses needed to strengthen the claim:**
- the early-birth (pregnancy) sensitivity analysis, pre-specified;
- results by parity group, since the question wording differs ("a" vs "another" child);
- weighted and design-based estimates;
- a check against the release that includes the errata corrections;
- an external cohort or later NLSY97 rounds (for example, expectations from Round 15 or 16) to test whether the recalibration transfers.

### 21.5 M05 warnings (`analysis/population_validity.py`)

**Reproduced** with `python -W default -m unittest tests.test_population_validity` (5 tests OK). A read-only diagnostic then ran the frozen functions with every warning recorded.

**Line 167, mean of empty slice.**
- **Trigger:** `MissingOutcomeTests.test_selection_table_reports_unknown_minus_known`.
- **Cause:** the synthetic frame has 100 households, 10 of them holding all the unknown outcomes. One of the 1,000 household-bootstrap replicates drew none of them (probability about 0.9^100 per replicate). `np.nanmean` over an empty unknown group returned NaN for that replicate.
- **Handling:** `np.nanpercentile` then ignores it, as the code comment at line 182 states.

**Line 185, divide by zero.**
- **Trigger:** the same test.
- **Cause:** by design, the synthetic frame gives every known woman age 27 and every unknown woman age 29. The pooled within-group SD is therefore 0, and the standardised mean difference for age is infinite.
- **Test coverage:** the test checks only the difference and its interval.

**Line 216, RankWarning.**
- **Trigger:** `CalibrationMeasureTests.test_weights_change_the_average`.
- **Cause:** the forecasts are constant (0.5, 0.5), so `np.polyfit` is rank-deficient and the slope is arbitrary.
- **Test coverage:** the test checks only the weighted average.

**Real data.**
- The full `python -m analysis.population_validity` run on the frozen data raised **no warnings**.
- In both cohorts, no bootstrap replicate lacked known or unknown women, and no column was all-missing in any replicate.
- No pooled SD was zero or missing.
- The printed tables contain no infinite values. The only NaN row is the 2-woman "baseline roster unusable" group, which the methodology already excludes. The other "nan" matches are the substring of "pregnant".
- The published selection-table values (for example stated chance +0.8 [−4.3, +5.6], SMD 0.02; age +0.26 [+0.08, +0.45], SMD 0.18) reproduce exactly.

**Conclusion.** The warnings arise only in the expected edge cases built into the synthetic tests. They affect no scientific result. The frozen file was not modified.

## 22. Final scientific closure and freeze of the synthesis (2026-11-20)

**Approval.** The independent source-package review accepted the scientific synthesis, the frozen research findings, the controlled M11 revision and the project-wide audit. It asked for bounded corrections, then a formal freeze (directive "The Intention Gap | Final Scientific Closure").

**Wording corrections** (manuscript, executive summary, limitations register, completion record, claim matrix, README, evidence-map figure). No number changed.
- **Population.** M09 results are restricted to U.S. adults aged about 18–27 in 2022–2023, the older part of Generation Z (born about 1995–2005). They are not generalized to all of Generation Z.
- **Prediction.** Categorical statements that demographics "did not" or "cannot" predict the explicit "no" were replaced by the supported finding: the evaluated models failed to demonstrate meaningful out-of-sample improvement over a class-prior baseline (holdout AUC 0.51 against 0.50). This does not show that demographics are unrelated to intentions.
- **Completion.** "Nearly complete by historical standards" is distinguished from completed lifetime fertility throughout. No projection to 2040 is described as completed fertility.
- **Data requirements.** The data needed for a population-average desired-versus-achieved difference (a numeric desired family size and completed cohort fertility) are distinguished from the stronger requirement for individual fulfilment (linked longitudinal observations).
- **Sterility.** Sterility statements now describe the CDC recode classification: respondents not asked the intention item because they or their partner were reported sterile. They no longer say the gap "reflects fecundity, not preference" or "is not a choice".
- **Forecasting.** "No method beat a no-change forecast" was replaced by "no alternative model satisfied the locked model-selection criteria".
